Strategic Summary — April 2026

April tells a clearer story than March. Total business revenue of $70,659 is the first month below the $87,500 monthly pace requirement. March's $99,133 was inflated by a large consulting settlement — April is a more representative read of where the business actually sits. Consulting at approximately $65,197 is solid but not exceptional, and without that settlement it confirms the business is running at roughly 75% of revenue pace. That gap is manageable if product and course revenue starts contributing — but right now it is not.

Product and course revenue in April was negligible on a sold basis: Wix shop $365, Vetr sold $280, aXcelerate $36. Five email campaigns sent, zero attributed orders for the second consecutive month. LinkedIn is performing strongly as a thought-leadership channel — clicks up 72% on March, engagement rate averaging 8.6% — but it is not yet converting to transactions. The pattern is consistent across both months: people-first and compliance-timed content (retreat photos 16.6% ER, team history post 14.5% ER, ASQA post 10.7% ER) drives engagement, while product posts and course emails drive sessions but not purchases. The audience is warm and engaged. The conversion mechanism is missing. Update as of 1 May: AC1 (The VET Landscape) is now live. The remaining AC2–AC12 units are in accelerated production with an offshore contractor. The EOFY sale (35% off everything, opening 1 June) includes AC1 and a full series pre-sale — this is the first real course enrolment opportunity for the coaching program.

Two flags require resolution before May spend is committed. First, the Xero Promotions and Advertising line shows $1,514.24 for April while Google Ads actual spend was $301.90. The $1,212 gap needs to be identified — this could be Meta ads, Canva subscriptions, or other platform costs being coded to this line. Until it is clarified, the advertising budget picture for April is incomplete. Second, marketing hours are at 73.5 hours for the second consecutive month well above the 20-hour cap. The Wix course page build (5.5 hrs), About Us page (3.2 hrs), and employee profile work (1.8 hrs) are development and operations tasks — not marketing promotion. Jess's 31.3 hours as a new Teamwork entrant also needs context: is she a contractor engaged specifically for marketing asset creation, or is her time being partially miscoded?
